The architecture in one paragraph

This platform is the opinionated KPI universe BEE recommends for growth measurement. It is structured as a pyramid (three tiers of decision rights) crossed by a funnel (seven stages of buyer progression). Every KPI sits in exactly one cell of the grid — at one tier and one stage. The structure is deliberate: it forces every metric to declare its role, its cadence, and its audience.

The pyramid — three tiers of decision rights

Each tier owns a different cadence and a different audience. The lower tier always supplies the diagnostic depth that explains movement in the tier above it.

Tier 1 Management Macro outcomes and North Stars · monthly to quarterly · board view
Tier 2 Expert Decision-grade diagnostics · weekly to monthly · function leaders
Tier 3 Optimizer Granular operational levers · daily to weekly · practitioners

No KPI repeats across tiers. If a metric "feels" like it belongs at two levels, it is in fact two different KPIs — one is the board-level aggregate, the other is the operational decomposition.

The funnel — seven stages, left to right

Every KPI sits in exactly one funnel stage. Movement at any stage cascades to the next, which is why diagnostic chains run forward from root cause to symptom.

Priority framework — MoSCoW anchored to the North Star hierarchy

Every KPI carries a priority of P1 (Must), P2 (Should), or P3 (Could). The assignment is not arbitrary — it follows a defensible rule.

  • P1 (Must) = Management-row North Stars plus the Expert leading indicators that directly gate a Management metric. ~15–18 KPIs.
  • P2 (Should) = Expert decision-grade KPIs that diagnose bottlenecks affecting a P1 metric. ~30–35 KPIs.
  • P3 (Could) = Optimizer-level operational levers, used when a P2 KPI flags a problem. ~30–35 KPIs.

RICE and ICE were rejected for this platform because their Reach / Effort / Confidence inputs are client-specific and would need to be invented for a baseline reference. MoSCoW provides a defensible three-bucket vocabulary that maps cleanly onto the platform's three priority filters.

Each KPI carries definition, meaning, and impact

Every entry in the glossary on Page 1 has three fields that together describe placement, value, and downstream dynamics:

  • Definition — what the KPI mathematically or operationally is. The unambiguous formula.
  • Meaning — what it signals about the business when it moves. The interpretation a leader should reach for.
  • Impact — what downstream KPIs and decisions it affects. The propagation map across the architecture.

Together these three fields make every KPI self-explaining — you should not need a colleague to decode what a number on the dashboard means.

Signals — special-purpose flags on certain KPI clusters

Five badges mark KPI clusters that share a special operating concern. They are visual cues, not separate categories.

  • Mktg ↔ Sales Review — red dot on KPIs that surface Marketing–Sales definitional drift before it shows up in pipeline. Hovering the dot reveals the specific handoff concern for that KPI.
  • GEO — Generative Engine Optimization. Visibility inside LLM answers and AI search surfaces.
  • AEO — Answer Engine Optimization. Content structured to be lifted directly into AI answers and trusted as a source.
  • SRA — Sales-Ready Account. Lives only at the Expert tier in Lead Conversion. Marks the threshold between raw leads and ICP accounts ready for sales outreach.
  • ABM — Account-Based Marketing. Named-account targeting and reach KPIs.
  • MIG — Site Migration Error Rate. Lives only at the Optimizer tier in AI / Organic Visibility. Dormant outside migration windows, critical during them.
  • LOOP — RevOps Feedback Loop. KPIs that close the loop between Sales outcomes and upstream Marketing decisions.

Tool boundaries — which tool measures which surface

Seven software sources cover the architecture. The split is deliberate — each tool owns part of the surface that the others cannot reach.

  • HubSpot (HS) — CRM-joined data: contacts, MQL / SQL, deals, revenue, attribution.
  • Peec AI (PAI) — LLM visibility: brand citations in AI answers, sentiment, prompt coverage.
  • Guard Audit (GA) — technical GEO crawl and schema validation.
  • Google Search Console (GSC) — organic Google search only. Does not measure Google Ads.
  • Google Ads (GAds) — platform-native paid search and display.
  • LinkedIn Ads (LI) — LinkedIn Campaign Manager metrics, member demographics.
  • Meta Ads (MA) — Facebook + Instagram paid metrics.

The full per-tool limit statements live on Page 4. The most important boundary to remember: GSC measures organic Google only. Paid Google data lives in Google Ads. The two tools are not interchangeable.

How the cause-effect chain works

On Page 3, the chain is read forward — left to right. The root cause sits on the left; intermediate diagnostics flow rightward; the symptom sits on the right. The symptom is the metric that moves first when the root cause is real — it is the cheapest tripwire, not the most important number.

A chain is only published when its path is defensible from real growth or RevOps practice. KPIs without a published chain show the "chain not yet defined" empty state rather than a fabricated diagnostic path. The Page 3 selector groups KPIs into "with chain" and "without chain yet" so you can see at a glance which KPIs are diagnosable today.
